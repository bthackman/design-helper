# Design drivers — Cairn Youth Centre (RAIC 400)

**Status: not yet locked.** Per this project's process-order adaptation (instructor requires
client → precedents → site, not the tool's default site-before-drivers order — see
`02_Decision-Log.md`), the drivers below are a **working/provisional set drafted to frame

3–7 big ideas that steer every phase. Each phase output gets tested against these. Keep them few, specific, and testable — "daylight to every workspace" not "good design."

## Working drivers (draft v0.1 — provisional, unlocked)

| # | Driver | Evidence tier | Testable form (can this fail?) | Origin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | **Built to rotate** *(restated 2026-09-28; was "Never fully off")*. The building runs two nested rotations: within each 4-week summer cycle one pod empties each week while its cohort camps, and across the year the residential wing switches on (summer, breaks, some weekends) and off (school-term nights). The old "never off" premise came from a crisis bed open every night, which has been dropped. | Structural (derived from the annual operating model) | Candidate test (from OQ 7): each pod is independently operable — its own threshold, zoned services, no through-traffic — so one pod can sit empty while two stay occupied, and the whole wing can close without darkening the public side. Stone Day (a stone added to the entry cairn at each graduation) is a candidate for making the rotation visible. Fails if the residential wing reads as one static dorm block. | `00_Brief.md` Annual operating model; `04_Client-Profile.md` row 3, row 6, "Ideas under consideration" |
| W2 | **Method follows setting.** Indoor spaces are tech-forward (robotics, computing, engineering); the landscape/outdoor program is field-science (environmental studies, biology) during camping weeks. The subject deliberately changes with the setting, not just the activity. | Stated (Ben's direct instruction on science sub-focus) | Fails if the outdoor basecamp/teaching yard is just "the indoor curriculum, outside" rather than teaching something the labs structurally can't — should have its own pedagogical identity (specimen work, field study, real ecological engagement), not be an overflow space. | `02_Decision-Log.md` science sub-focus decision |
| W3 | **One front door, two postures.** The building has to read as a public, bookable destination for schools, families and donors *and* a protected residential home — simultaneously, without either posture spilling into the other. *(Revised 2026-09-28: no crisis intake; the sanctuary suite is a private step-away room inside the residential wing.)* | Contradiction (the client profile's sharpest named tension) | Fails if a visitor in the public zone can see into a pod or the sanctuary suite, or can tell who is staying there. Passes if one entry sequence does three emotional jobs — celebratory cohort arrival, routine day-visitor drop-off, alumni homecoming — while staff shift changes and residential movement use a quieter second entrance. | `04_Client-Profile.md` rows 1, 5, 7, 8, and "Contradictions worth designing around" #1–2 |
| W4 | **Risk, provided safely — not risk, removed.** The org's governance instinct is to minimize risk; the mission (independence via real wilderness consequence) needs real risk preserved. The weather/smoke resilience system (basecamp + flex hall) is the architecture's answer to that tension, not a workaround bolted on afterward. | Contradiction (client profile row 11 + row 1) | Fails if the resilience spaces read as a diminished, "consolation prize" version of camping. Passes if the basecamp/flex-hall fallback is designed with enough conviction that staff would defend it as a real alternative, not an apology for a cancelled trip — and if it structurally preserves pod vacancy either way (see the scheduling math already locked). | `02_Decision-Log.md` weather/smoke resilience decision; `04_Client-Profile.md` row 11 |
| W5 | **Human scale, every grain.** Cohorts are capped at 6, classrooms are mentorship-scale, and the summer age band was deliberately narrowed (12–15) partly *to keep one simple, legible supervision model* rather than tiered institutional protocols. No room the population directly occupies should read as institutional-sized, even where shared infrastructure (kitchen, flex hall) genuinely needs to be larger. | Derived (from cohort-size, age-band, and classroom decisions taken together) | Fails if any dorm, classroom, or common space reads at "camp bunkhouse" or "school corridor" scale rather than small-group scale. The large shared spaces (kitchen/dining, flex hall) are allowed to be big — the test is whether the *population-facing* rooms stay small even when the building around them doesn't. | `02_Decision-Log.md` cohort-size and age-range decisions; `00_Brief.md` program table (classroom/dorm unit sizes) |

## Consolidation workshop (run once, at the drivers-lock gate — see `phase-0-drivers.md` §4)
Every candidate driver tested against the verified site facts, one row each, before anything locks.
Full method in `phase-0-drivers.md`; this is just the table it currently has no template home.

| Candidate driver | Evidence tier (stated/derived/structural/contradiction) | Site fact tested against | Verdict | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  | CONFIRM / SPECIFY / DEFLATE-REFRAME / DEMOTE |  |

**Verdict** — CONFIRM (site strengthens it, lock as-is) · SPECIFY (site adds precision, lock
sharpened) · DEFLATE-REFRAME (site makes part of it moot, salvage what survives) · DEMOTE (right
idea, wrong scale — send to the relevant phase as a quality target, not a project driver).
**Disposition** — where it actually went: locked as-is / locked-reframed-into [driver #] /
demoted-to [phase] / dropped-because [reason]. Nothing on this table may be left blank — the "full
disposition" lock-quality gate means every candidate's fate is traceable, not just the winners'.
