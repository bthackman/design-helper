# Brief — RAIC400 Youth Science Centre

*Version: 0.4 · Date: 2026-09-22 · Source: RAIC 400 course guidelines (rubric/reference only) + Ben's own program direction, interrogated*

## Project summary
A "Collective Habitat" studio project for RAIC 400 (Foundations of Architectural Design). Mission: a youth centre serving children/youth in troubled situations — the same population YOUCAN's sample brief targets — but using **academics and science as the intervention vehicle**, not conventional counseling/social-services programming alone. The building runs on **two nested rotations, same organizing principle at two scales**: a weekly pod rotation within the summer cohort program, and a seasonal rhythm across the year (full residential in summer, day-use in the school year). This dual-scale rotation is a candidate design driver — see `02_Decision-Log.md`. Location, scale, cohort size, and age range served are not yet fixed — see `03_Open-Questions.md`.

| **Year-round, cross-cutting** | **Confirmed:** a dedicated 1–2 bed sanctuary suite (not one of the 3 main pods) for the most severe cases needing overnight refuge from home, available even during a normal school week | Crisis-intake individual kids, not cohorts | Presumably within the 8–15 overall range | Yes, atypical — requires a live-in/overnight duty youth-care worker on staff, a role no other part of the program needs | N/A or minimal | A separate small suite, near staff/counseling wing |

**Weather/wildfire-smoke resilience (Jul–Aug camping weeks):** dual on-site fallback — outdoor basecamp (fire circle, low-ropes elements, outdoor teaching yard) **or** the indoor flexible hall/gymnasium, staff's call based on conditions that week. Either fallback must **preserve pod vacancy** — the cohort still leaves its normal pod for the week — or the 3-pod staggered schedule collapses during exactly the months (Jul–Aug) when wildfire smoke risk is highest. Building systems get only a general mention (e.g. "resilient, filterable envelope") per Ben's call — specifying exact filtration standards (e.g. MERV ratings) would exceed the level this design exercise is asking for.

## Constraints
- Course constraints: instructor-reviewed (not code-enforced as a real permit set), medium-scale per the course's own framing, must demonstrate hierarchical spaces (private→semi-private→public), three distinct circulation classes (public/open, semi-public/restricted, private/closed), long-span/short-span program rationalization, and general (not detailed) building-systems integration.
- Deliverable cadence: Design Brief → Schematic Design (interim review) → Final presentation, plus a mandatory sketchbook throughout.
- Grading: three weighted components per assignment (Ideas / Connections / Extensions).
- Site, budget, schedule: not yet fixed (OQ #3).
