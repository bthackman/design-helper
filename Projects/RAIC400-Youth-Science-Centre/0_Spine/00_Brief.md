# Brief — Cairn Youth Centre: Academics & Outdoors (RAIC 400)

*Version: 0.6 · Date: 2026-09-28 · Source: RAIC 400 course guidelines (rubric/reference only) + Ben's own program direction, interrogated. Client: Cairn Youth Centre (hypothetical), see `04_Client-Profile.md`.*

## Project summary
A "Collective Habitat" studio project for RAIC 400 (Foundations of Architectural Design). Mission: a youth centre serving kids in tough situations at home and kids from low-income families — the same population YOUCAN's sample brief targets — using **academics, making and the outdoors as the intervention vehicle**, not conventional counselling/social-services programming alone. It is an academic building with maker spaces, not a public science centre. The building runs on **two nested rotations**: a cohort rotation within each four-week summer cycle (one pod empty each week while its cohort is camping), and a seasonal switch across the year (full residential in summer and breaks, day use in the school year). Settled: Calgary, 18 beds in 3 pods of 6, summer ages 12–15, school-year ages 8–15, about 2,480 m² gross. Still open: the site — see `03_Open-Questions.md`.

## Staffing model (2026-09-28)
Most staff work shifts and go home. **Only the chaperones sleep over**, in their suites, during residential programs. Admin, kitchen, educators and night staff all work shifts.

| Group | Headcount | When | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leadership and admin | about 6 | Year-round, day shifts | Executive director, program director, facilities and operations manager, outdoor and expedition coordinator, intake and family liaison, admin support |
| Counsellors | 3 | Year-round | One office each |
| Educators | 2 | Year-round | Run the labs, maker space and classrooms. New 2026-09-28 |
| Kitchen | about 2 | Shifts, scaled to the program | Full service in summer; snack and lunch in the school term |
| Chaperones | 5 | Residential programs, live-in | One per pod, one expedition lead, plus one relief chaperone for days off. Up from 4. The relief shares the rota, so there are still 4 suites |
| Youth workers | about 4 | Summer, day and evening shifts | New 2026-09-28. Extra hands for programming, meals and supervision |
| Night staff | 5–6 | **Summer peaks only**, awake overnight shifts | Work from the night staff station. How nights are covered during breaks and overnight weekend camps is still to confirm (OQ 8) |

Year-round core is about 11 (13 with kitchen). At the summer peak it's about 27–28 people across all shifts. **Space implication:** shift staff need lockers, a change room and a shower (about 10 m²), to add at space planning. The 65 m² open workroom works as shared hot-desking.

**Weather/wildfire-smoke resilience (Jul–Aug camping weeks):** dual on-site fallback — outdoor basecamp (fire circle, low-ropes elements, outdoor teaching yard) **or** the indoor flexible hall/gymnasium, staff's call based on conditions that week. Either fallback must **preserve pod vacancy** — the cohort still leaves its normal pod for the week — or the 3-pod staggered schedule collapses during exactly the months (Jul–Aug) when wildfire smoke risk is highest. Building systems get only a general mention (e.g. "resilient, filterable envelope") per Ben's call — specifying exact filtration standards (e.g. MERV ratings) would exceed the level this design exercise is asking for.

## Site requirements (2026-09-24)
Exterior scope, not in the building totals. Stall counts to be set by City of Calgary bylaw once a site is chosen.
- **Arrival and parking:** school bus drop-off (sheltered lay-by); kids' drop-off zone (pull-through loop at the front door, separate from the bus, also serves summer intake every four weeks); van fleet parking for 5–10 vans next to the garage bay (course sample); staff parking for ~15 on a peak day (estimate); small visitor lot, ~5 stalls incl. one accessible.
- **Service and loading:** kitchen delivery loading at shipping/receiving, short run to the walk-in; concealed waste/recycling enclosure reachable by a collection truck (composting ties into field science); garage overhead door onto a service drive; service route never crosses kids' arrival or play.
- **Outdoors:** outdoor basecamp (fire circle, low-ropes, teaching yard); field-science ground with real ecology on or next to the site; play field/yard for day-camp groups.
- **Access and location:** close to transit; a quieter second entrance to the residential side (staff shift changes, residential arrivals) that avoids the public zone. Revised 2026-09-28: there is no late-night crisis intake any more. **Tension:** course sample asks for a central location; precedents argue for land with real ecology.

## Constraints
- Course constraints: instructor-reviewed (not code-enforced as a real permit set), medium-scale per the course's own framing, must demonstrate hierarchical spaces (private→semi-private→public), three distinct circulation classes (public/open, semi-public/restricted, private/closed), long-span/short-span program rationalization, and general (not detailed) building-systems integration.
- Deliverable cadence: Design Brief → Schematic Design (interim review) → Final presentation, plus a mandatory sketchbook throughout.
- Grading: three weighted components per assignment (Ideas / Connections / Extensions).
- Site: Stage 1 zone scan drafted 2026-09-25, not yet chosen (OQ #3, #10, #11). Budget, schedule: not yet fixed.
