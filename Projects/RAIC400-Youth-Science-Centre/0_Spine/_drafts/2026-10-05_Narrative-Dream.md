## 1. The narrative

### In one paragraph
Cairn is for academically inclined city kids, 12 to 16, from across Canada and around the world. Most of them are good at school and new to the outdoors. Many have never been away from home. Cairn treats the whole stay as one expedition, and it starts the day they leave home. The flight or the long coach ride is the first step. The first night in a strange bed is the second. From there the kids are walked, one step at a time, from comfort toward exposure: building things in the workshop, testing them in the yard, riding into the city, sleeping out on the site, and finally going into the wilderness with what they built. They go as a team of eight. They come back up the same steps, warmer and drier at each one, and leave a stone on the cairn. The building and its site are that gradient made physical. Every step is a real place, and each place is a little harder than the last.

### The story: Ana's two weeks
*Written for the 2-week session with paired trips (Section 5). Ana is invented. Node names (stone types) are an idea, not a decision.*

**Day 0, Sunday. Leaving.** Ana is 13 and lives on the 22nd floor of a condo in Toronto. She has never slept anywhere without a parent down the hall. Her mother hugs her at the airport. She sits beside a girl from her class she doesn't really know. Four hours later the plane drops over flat brown fields and she sees the mountains for the first time, a grey line on the left.

**Arrival.** A Cairn coach is waiting. Forty minutes later it pulls into a gravel loop. The first thing she sees is a pile of stones taller than her, by the front door. A guide says every kid who has ever come here added one. Inside, it is warm. There is a bench for boots, a wall of hooks, and a long wall covered in charts and photos. Her name is on a tag on a cubby. She is in Shale node: two four-bed rooms, a washroom, and her teacher's door at the end of the hall.

**First night.** Dinner is loud. Afterwards the four nodes split up, and Shale goes to the hearth room: low ceiling, a stove, cushions, a window toward the dark field. A guide asks everyone to say one thing they are scared of. Ana says bears. A boy from Lyon says the toilets. Everyone laughs. She texts her mother a photo of the stove. In bed she can hear the wind. The night lamp in the hall stays on.

**Days 1–2. The workshop and the site.** Morning: a walk around the site. The guide points out the yard, the fire circle, a ring of tent platforms, a small outhouse, and, farther out, the trail. "By next week you'll have slept out there." Afternoon: the workshop. The brief is a fire-starting tool for a wet day. Shale builds a wind shield out of folded aluminium and a tinder box that stays dry. They test it in the yard in a drizzle. It fails twice. The third version works.

**Day 3. First mini expedition.** Bikes from the bike room. A helmet check, a flat-tire lesson, then the whole node rides the pathway into the city and back. Ana hasn't ridden since she was nine. By the afternoon her legs ache and she has fixed someone else's chain. Shale eats lunch in a park looking at the downtown towers, which suddenly feel very far away.

**Days 4–5. Build and test.** Shale designs its dining shelter: a tarp, poles, a ridge line, and a way to keep the wind off the stove. They pitch it in the yard, take it down, and pitch it again faster. Then the raft: barrels, lashings, a deck of lashed poles. Everyone wears a life jacket. A guide sits in a canoe beside them. The raft lists to one corner and Ana gets wet to the waist. They fix the lashing and it floats level. The data from the water sampling goes on the lab bench.

**Night 5. Sleep-out on the site.** Shale and one other node sleep on the platforms beyond the yard. They cook on the fire circle with their own tool. At 2 a.m. it starts to rain and the tarp sags. Ana and two others get up and re-tension it in the dark. She can see the lit window of the night station across the field. She doesn't go in.

**Days 6–8. The expedition.** A van to the mountains. Two nights out with two guides. Real rain. A cold camp. Cooking is slow, the bathroom is a trowel, her socks never dry. The dining shelter holds. The fire tool works on the second morning when nothing else does. On day 7 they cross a ridge and she can see a valley with no buildings in it at all.

**Day 8. Coming back up.** The van drops them at the trail door, not the front door. Boots go on the wash rack. Wet gear goes straight into the drying room. Hot showers. A hot meal saved for them. That night the hearth room is the best room she has ever been in. The comfort feels earned.

**Days 9–11. Turning around.** Now Shale is the experienced node. They brief the two nodes that are leaving: how to pitch the shelter in wind, which socks to bring. Ana helps them pack. In the lab Shale adds its stream and weather data to the field-record wall. One more trip into the city: the science centre, then the university. Two days later the other nodes come back through the trail door, muddy, and Shale has hot chocolate waiting.

**Day 12. Stone Day.** A showcase in the hall for teachers, staff and a few neighbours: the fire tool, the raft, the shelter, the data. At the end, each kid takes a stone to the cairn by the front door. Ana writes nothing on hers. She just puts it on top.

**Day 13. Home.** The flight east. Her mother meets her at the airport. That week the power goes out in the condo. Ana is the one who finds the candles, and she doesn't mind the dark.

## 2. The ladder: the spine of the parti

Comfort level is a rough 1–5 (5 = home, 1 = the wilderness). "Step" means the parti's named rungs. Steps 0–8 go down; steps 9–10 come back up. Every rung must be a real place, and each rung must be harder than the one before.

| # | Step | What the kid does | What toughens | What the building or site must provide | In / out | Comfort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Leaving home** | Flight or long coach ride, often alone with classmates | Separation, independence, managing own bag and passport | Off-site. The coach loop and luggage route must work for 32 + bags | Out (travel) | 4 |
| 1 | **Arrival** | Arrives, drops bags, boots off, finds name tag and node | First impression: "a trip begins at the front door" | Arrival door, coach bay, arrival room with luggage space and cubbies, the cairn, the field-record wall | In | 5 |
| 2 | **First night** | Node dinner, hearth room, first sleep away | Homesickness, sleeping with strangers | Warm, small, low rooms: the hearth room, pods of 4, the teacher's suite next door, the night station's light | In | 5 |
| 3 | **Workshop** | Designs and builds kit: fire tool, shelter, raft | Problem solving, teamwork, failure and retry | Clean workshop (electronics, 3D printers) and heavy workshop (wood, rope, metal) opening straight onto the yard | In | 4 |
| 4 | **Yard** | Tests kit in the weather, pitches shelters, fire circle by day | First weather exposure with a warm room 10 m away | Build and test yard, covered build shed, fire circle, outdoor kitchen, close to the heavy workshop | Out, near | 3–4 |
| 5 | **City mini expedition** | Day bike ride; transit and city day on foot | Endurance, route finding, group pace, fixing a flat | Bike store and repair bench; a direct route to a pathway; the city within riding range (site-dependent) | Out, urban | 3 |
| 6 | **Water** | Raft trial on still water, sampling | A chosen, supervised risk; getting wet and cold | Still, supervised water; a launch or dock; life-jacket store; wash-down on the way back. Moving water stays off the route | Out, near | 2–3 |
| 7 | **Sleep-out on site** | One night on platforms; cooks on fire; outhouse | Rough sleeping, night weather, bathroom, cooking | Sleep-out ground within sight of the night station, 100–200 m from the building `[assumption]`; tent platforms; composting toilet; hand pump or carried water | Out, on site | 2 |
| 8 | **Wilderness expedition** | 2 nights (school year) or ~5 days (summer) in the mountains | Everything harder: wet, cold, fire, food, bathroom, distance | Off-site (Kananaskis or similar, outside design scope). Building provides the packing room, gear store, garage and vans | Out, far | 1 |
| 9 | **Return** | Through the trail door; wash, dry, shower, hot meal; debrief | Recovery; seeing the comfort as earned | Trail door, gear wash, drying room, showers on the trail side, a meal held in the dining room, the hearth room | In | 5 |
| 10 | **Hand-off and Stone Day** | Briefs the next pair, adds data to the field record, showcase, stone on the cairn | Leadership, reflection, mentoring the next group | Lab, field-record wall, hall for the showcase, the cairn at the arrival door (the ladder ends where it began) | In / out | 5 |

**Two things the ladder makes clear.**
- **The gradient is also a thermal and climatic gradient.** Steps 1–2 are fully heated, steps 3 and 9 are tempered work rooms, step 4 is roofed but unheated, steps 5–7 are open. That is the building-systems story in outline (Section 3).
- **Stepping back is part of the design.** If the expedition is cancelled for smoke or weather, the node steps back one rung (an extra night on the sleep-out ground, or the yard and the hall), not off the ladder. This is W4's fallback restated in the parti's terms.

**Sources and practice behind the ladder.** All cited from memory and not re-checked this session; verify before the board.
- Walsh, V. & Golins, G. (1976), *The Exploration of the Outward Bound Process*, Colorado Outward Bound School: the learner is placed in an unfamiliar physical setting with a small group and given problem-solving tasks of increasing difficulty. This is the closest academic match to the ladder. `[unverified]`
- Hattie, J., Marsh, H. W., Neill, J. T. & Richards, G. E. (1997), "Adventure education and Outward Bound: Out-of-class experiences that make a lasting difference", *Review of Educational Research* 67(1): meta-analysis finding positive effects on self-concept and related outcomes, some lasting after the program. `[unverified]`
- Thurber, C. A. & Walton, E. A. (2007), "Preventing and treating homesickness", *Pediatrics* 119(1): homesickness is common in children away from home; prevention includes practice time away and a gradual, supported start. Supports steps 1–2 as designed rooms, not afterthoughts. `[unverified]`
- The comfort / stretch / panic zone model (often credited to Senninger, 2000) and "challenge by choice" (Project Adventure) are common outdoor-education framings for staged challenge. `[unverified]`

## 3. How the parti becomes architecture

The parti has to cross the course's privacy hierarchy (private → semi-private → public, Guidelines p. 9). They are not the same axis. The most comfortable rooms (the nodes) are the most private; the most exposed places (the land) are semi-public; the public side (gym, arrival) sits at the road. So every reading needs **two axes: privacy, and toughness**.

```
              PRIVACY  (course)
   public ───────────── semi-private ───────────── private
   road, hall, arrival   dining, workshop, lab      nodes, hearth

              TOUGHNESS  (parti)
   comfort ──────── tempered ──────── roofed ──────── open ──── wild
   nodes, hearth    workshop, gear    build shed      yard, sleep-out   expedition
```

### Reading A: the line (bench to trail)
One spine from the road to the land. Arrival and the public hall at the road end. The nodes and hearth branch off on the warm side. The spine then runs dining → workshop → expedition zone → trail door → yard → sleep-out ground → trail.
```
 ROAD ─ arrival + hall ─┬─ dining ─ workshop ─ expedition zone ─ TRAIL DOOR ─ yard ─ sleep-out ─ trail →
                        └─ hearth ─ nodes (private branch)
```
- **Pros:** the most legible diagram; carries the earlier candidate A ("Build it, then live with it") as the organizing line; W3's two doors fall at each end; easy to show the three circulation classes (public at the road end, semi-public along the spine, private on the branch).
- **Cons:** long, so more circulation (the 1.30 grossing factor is at risk); needs a deep site; the nodes are off to the side of the parti rather than in it.

### Reading B: hearth and rings
A warm core (hearth, nodes, dining) wrapped by a working ring (workshop, lab, expedition zone), wrapped by the yard ring (shed, fire circle, outdoor kitchen), then the land. The public hall sits on the outer ring at the road.
```
          land / sleep-out
       ┌── yard ring ─────────┐
       │  ┌ working ring ┐    │
  hall │  │  HEARTH +    │    │
 (road)│  │  NODES       │    │
       │  └──────────────┘    │
       └──────────────────────┘
```
- **Pros:** compact (good for the grossing factor and a tight site); a strong winter story, since the warmest rooms are literally sheltered by the working rooms; a courtyard reading that suits the precedents (Aranya's ring gallery).
- **Cons:** no direction, so no "far end" and no single trail door; the expedition would leave from any side; the public side has to push through rings or be bolted on.

### Reading C: building to land (the site holds the steps)
A compact building on the road edge holds steps 1–3 and 9–10. The site is organized in bands of increasing distance and wildness: terrace → yard → shelter ground → sleep-out ground → trail. On a sloping site, the building steps down the slope so that arrival and the nodes are on top and the trail door is at the bottom (a vertical variant).
```
 ROAD │ BUILDING (comfort) │ terrace │ yard + shed │ shelter ground │ sleep-out │ → trail / water
      │  nodes up, workshop down (on a slope)                                     (farther = rougher)
```
- **Pros:** answers the instructor's site–client point directly: the site's parameters are the steps; makes each site's gradient comparable in the test fits; keeps the building simple; the stepped section literally steps.
- **Cons:** the building carries less of the parti and risks being "a box with a landscape plan"; needs land (weak on A Inglewood, strong on C Loch McKinnon); far bands need supervision sight lines.

### Recommendation
**Reading A inside, Reading C outside.** The building is a short line from hearth to trail door (steps 2 → 3 → 9). The land carries steps 4–7 in bands. Use the stepped section where the site slopes (C Loch McKinnon's terrace falls about 8 m to the lake; D Glenbow's bench falls to the south). Keep Reading B as the fallback for a tight site.

### Course requirements, against the recommendation
| Requirement (Guidelines p. 9) | How the gradient carries it |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy of spaces | Pod of 4 → node lounge or hearth → dining and hall. The privacy hierarchy runs across the line; the toughness gradient runs along it. Draw both on one diagram. |
| Three circulation classes | Public: road → arrival → hall. Semi-public (restricted): arrival → dining → workshop → expedition zone → trail door. Private: the node corridor. Servicing: garage and kitchen loading on their own drive. The trail route is the semi-public line extended outdoors. |
| Long-span vs short-span | Long-span: hall, dining, heavy workshop and covered build shed. Short-span: pods, node washrooms, leader suites, offices. The span gets bigger as you move toward exposure, which is a nice coincidence worth stating. |
| Systems in outline | Thermal zoning follows the gradient: fully heated nodes, tempered workshop and expedition zone, unheated build shed, open yard. The drying room is the visible systems room (heat and exhaust). The envelope gets lighter toward the land. |
| Community interface | The hall and arrival face the road and open to neighbours (community nights, the showcase). The cairn is visible from the street. On a park site, the public trail passes the yard. |

## 5. Session length (OQ 28)

**The problem.** The v0.7 rotation sends one node out at a time, 2 nights each, back to back across days 3–11. That means the first node goes out after only two or three days, and the last node waits about nine. The build-up is unequal, and the first node never gets "about a week". The parti and W1 conflict as currently written.

| Option | How it runs | Build-up before the trip | Rotation (W1) | Studio and client read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1-week session** | Sun–Sat, 6 program days. Everyone builds up days 1–3, sleep-out night 3, all four nodes out together days 4–5 | ~3 days | Gone: all 32 out at once, ~8 guides, building empty | Matches typical school-trip length (3–5 days, per the 2026-09-29 cadence research), but too short to step down the ladder and back. Weak for kids flying in from abroad. The rotation and held-rooms idea disappears |
| **2-week, single-file (v0.7)** | One node out at a time, days 3–11 | 2–9 days, unequal | Kept as is | Building always ¾ full. But the first node skips most of the ladder |
| **2-week, paired** | Days 1–5 everyone builds up (workshop, yard, city ride, raft, sleep-out). Days 6–8 two nodes out; days 9–11 the other two. Day 12 Stone Day | 5 days for the first pair, 8 for the second | Rewritten: half out, half holding the base | Equal-enough build-up. The returning pair briefs the leaving pair (mentorship pillar). Needs about 4 guides out at once (outdoor staff 3 → 5). Building half empty for 5 nights, rooms still held |
| **Summer, 4-week** | Week 1 build-up for all; weeks 2–4 one node at a time on ~5-day trips | 7+ days | Single-file still works | Unchanged. The ladder fits easily |

**Recommendation (Ben decides): keep 2-week school-year sessions and switch to paired trips; keep the 4-week summer model.** It is the only school-year option where every kid gets most of the ladder before the wilderness. It keeps W1's core (a node's rooms stay held while it's out), changes "one in four out" to "two in four out", and adds a hand-off moment the story needs. Cost: more guides, and the expedition zone must pack two nodes at once (the 50 m² packing room should take 16 kids; check at test fit).

If Ben wants to keep single-file: shorten every trip to one night, start trips on day 5, and accept that the last node returns on Stone Day.

## 6. What it means for site selection (OQ 23, 24)

### What the narrative asks of a site
Each criterion restated as a client need, with the ladder step it serves:

| Client need | Step | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **Arrival that starts the trip well** | 0–1 | Coach time from YYC; a coach loop at the door |
| **The city within reach for mini expeditions** | 5 | A pathway from the door; a day ride to downtown, the zoo or the university is plausible; city destinations within a short coach trip |
| **Near-wild ground on the site** | 4, 7 | Room for yard, shed, fire circle and a sleep-out ground in sight of the building, without clearing protected land. Fires and overnight camping allowed (city parks restrict both, `[check with the City]`) |
| **Still, supervised water** | 6 | Still water the kids can reach under supervision, for the raft and sampling. Craft allowed |
| **Moving water kept out of daily life** | all | No river on the kids' daily routes or beside the nodes; the trail to the river (if any) goes through a supervised gate |
| **The far end not too far** | 8 | Drive to the mountain gateway (52–61 min for all four sites; does not separate them) |
| **A community to face** | 10 | Neighbours who can use the hall and see the cairn |

**Water, in one rule:** still, supervised water is a resource (step 6); moving or unsupervised water is a hazard. Where both exist, the best case is the still water between the building and the river, so the hazard sits beyond the resource and the kids meet the easier water first.

### The four sites against the narrative (brief, not a pick)
- **A Inglewood.** Best at the comfort and city end: 18 min from YYC, the Bow pathway at the door, the zoo about 1.8 km away. Weakest in the middle: the envelope is tight (0.94), so the yard and sleep-out ground would have to use the sanctuary lawn. Water: the Bow about 150 m away is moving water; the lagoon is still but inside a bird sanctuary, so no rafts. Reads as **city-strong, middle-weak**.
- **B Glenmore Landing.** The most even across the ladder: the reservoir pathway loop is a ready-made first ride, the forest gives sheltered near-wild ground, and the reservoir is still water with existing sailing and paddling clubs (the 2026-09-29 study names the Glenmore sailing school). Swimming is likely barred on a drinking-water reservoir, and a homemade raft on it is `[unverified]`. Best mountain time of the four (52 min). Risk: the forest is a mapped natural area, so sleeping out and fires in it are a policy question. Reads as **balanced, if the park allows the middle steps**.
- **C Loch McKinnon.** Strongest for the middle steps: 26 ha of dry private terrace holds the whole ladder on site, a still lake at the door, and the Bow beyond the lake (the hazard sits behind the resource). The terrace slope suits the stepped section. Weaker at the city end (29 min combined, almost no mapped path at the door) and rail noise at about 140 m. Lake water quality `[unverified]`. Reads as **middle-strong, city-weaker**.
- **D Glenbow Ranch.** The site is already near the far end, so it collapses the gradient: there is little comfort or city to step down from. No suitable still water near the facility zone (ponds `[check]`); the Bow is about 700 m away and 90 m below. Fragile fescue makes a sleep-out ground hard to justify; grass fire and one road out. Reads as **all exposure, no ladder**.

**What this suggests for OQ 25, without choosing:** the gradient needs both ends close by. A pure-city site lacks the middle; a pure-wild site lacks the start. **On the narrative alone, B and C fit best**, which differs from the deck's ranking (A first at 28). If Ben keeps A, the test fit has to show where steps 4, 6 and 7 go.

## 7. Drivers (draft, unlocked)

**What the narrative does to W1–W5.**
- **W1 Built to rotate: rewritten.** The node of 8 becomes a team that builds, travels and comes back together, not just a bed count. "One node out" becomes "a pair out" if paired trips are chosen.
- **W2 Method follows setting: rewritten.** The tech-inside / field-outside split no longer describes the program. The real link is make-it-then-use-it.
- **W3 Two doors: strengthened.** The arrival door is the comfort end; the trail door is the exposure end. Unchanged in substance.
- **W4 Risk, provided safely: strengthened and sharpened.** The ladder is the method; water gets a rule; the fallback becomes "step back one rung".
- **W5 Human scale: folded in.** Its test (small rooms the kids occupy) survives inside D1's warm end and D4's team grain. Demote it to a space-planning quality target.
- **Candidate "the site is the first expedition": absorbed** into D1 and the site criteria.
