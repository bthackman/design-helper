# Parti and narrative — draft (2026-10-05)

## 1. The narrative (Ben, 2026-10-05)

Cairn serves **academically inclined urban kids aged 12–15**, who may come from across Canada and around the world. It gives them an experience that develops their **engineering and problem-solving skills**, with one big component on top: a **wilderness expedition** that develops them further and builds confidence.

It is also strongly **social**: the kids have to work together.

The engineering and the wilderness meet. A node might **design its own dining shelter**, **build a raft and take it onto the reservoir**, or **engineer a tool that helps it start a fire**. What they make in the workshop, they then depend on outside.

And these urban kids develop a **toughness that only happens in the wilderness**: rough sleeping, wet or bad weather, starting fires, and the fact that everything is harder out there, from going to the bathroom to eating and cooking.

## 2. What this changes

- **Who:** "visiting students" becomes **academically inclined urban kids**. The urban part matters: the wilderness is new to them, so the toughening has to be staged.
- **The link between the two halves:** the maker lab is no longer just "two-thirds academics". It is where the kit for the expedition gets designed and built. This gives W2 (method follows setting) a hard test: *does what a node builds get used outside?*
- **Social:** the node of 8 is a team that builds and lives together, not just a bed count. This supports W1 and W5.
- **Site:** this answers the instructor's tie-the-site-to-the-client point directly. The site is where the kids first test what they build (the shelter in the yard, the raft on the water, the fire circle) and where the toughening starts, before the expedition.

## 3. Parti candidates (pick one, or combine)

**A. "Build it, then live with it."**
Every expedition starts at a workbench. The plan runs in a line from **workshop → yard → trail**: the maker lab opens straight onto a test yard, and the test yard opens onto the land. What's built inside is carried out the same door.
*Main parameter:* the lab-to-trail line. *Test:* can a node carry a raft or a shelter frame from the bench to the water without passing through the residential or public side?

**B. "A gradient of toughness."**
The building and site are arranged as a gradient from **comfort to exposure**. Arrival and the dorms are warm and urban. The workshop and the expedition zone are rougher. The yard, fire circle and shelter ground on the site are outdoors but close. The expedition is the far end. Across two weeks a node moves down the gradient and comes back up it. This is the instructor's tempering idea made spatial.
*Main parameter:* exposure, stepped. *Test:* every step is a real place on the plan or site, and each one is a little harder than the last.

**C. "The site is the first expedition."** (the candidate driver logged earlier today)
The land around the building is the training ground: the kids sleep out one night on site before going far, cook outdoors, and test their raft on the water. The building is base camp.
*Main parameter:* what the site offers as near-wild. *Test:* a node can do a full rehearsal (build, cook, sleep out, fire) without leaving the property.

**Claude's recommendation: B as the parti, with A as its organizing line.** B is the guiding light: Cairn exists to toughen urban kids in stages. A is how the plan delivers it: the bench, yard and trail are the middle steps of the gradient. C then becomes a site-selection criterion rather than a parti: *does the site offer the near-wild steps of the gradient?* That is the client-led site parameter the instructor asked for (OQ 23).

## 4. What it does to the site criteria (feeds OQ 23)

Each criterion restated as a client need:

| Old criterion | Restated as a client need |
|---|---|
| Landscape | **Near-wild ground for the middle steps:** room for a test yard, a fire circle, an on-site sleep-out and shelter builds, without leaving the property. Parkland tempers and acclimatizes the kids before the expedition. |
| Mountains | **The far end of the gradient:** how far the expedition ground is. |
| City | **Arrival and the urban kids' world:** the airport for kids flying in, and museums, U of C and city days on the academic side. |
| Room & flexibility | **Room for the whole gradient**, not just the building footprint. |
| Hazards | **Risk that's chosen versus risk that isn't:** a raft on still water under supervision is a chosen risk. A river with currents beside the dorms is not. Arterials and rail stay counted. |
| Coach / Services | Unchanged: the operation has to work. |

## 5. Water, reframed (feeds OQ 24)

The raft example changes the water question. Water is not simply a hazard to keep away. **Still, supervised water is a resource, and moving or unsupervised water is a hazard.** For each site, the test becomes:
1. Is there still water the kids can reach under supervision, for the raft and water sampling?
2. Is moving water (the Bow) within easy reach of the dorms or of the kids' daily routes?

A reservoir that bans swimming or boating (Glenmore's drinking-water reservoir, to be checked) would fail test 1.
