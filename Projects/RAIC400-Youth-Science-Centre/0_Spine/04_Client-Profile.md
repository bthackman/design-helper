# Client profile (organization) — Cairn Youth Centre (RAIC 400)

**Mode: school/hypothetical.** Every row below is invented, traced to evidence already established in `00_Brief.md` and `02_Decision-Log.md` — not a real organization. Revised 2026-09-28 after a realism and consistency review; the earlier "Trailhead" profile and the crisis-bed sanctuary are superseded (history in `02_Decision-Log.md`).

## Cairn at a glance (confirmed by Ben)
- **Name:** Cairn Youth Centre, subtitle "Academics & Outdoors". An academic building with maker spaces and a STEM focus, **not** a public science centre. The program also builds **character and confidence**.
- **Founder:** Rocky Pyle (invented, deliberately jokey — a cairn is a rock pile), engineer and entrepreneur. As a kid Rocky attended a charity-funded summer camp (Ben's reference: the Tim Hortons Foundation Camps model) and found solace in academic work, which led to engineering, then to founding Cairn. His role today (executive director, or founding board chair with a hired director) is still open (OQ 13).
- **History:** relatively new. Ran as a summer camp in rented space for a few summers; its success led to building a full year-round facility. The summer camp is still the anchor program.
- **Location:** within the City of Calgary. Kids arrive with family, through Children's Services, or on their own by transit.
- **Who it serves:** kids in tough situations at home **and** kids from low-income families.
- **Mission:** "Cairn gives young people facing hard circumstances a place to build real skills, and the character and confidence to use them, in the lab, the workshop and the wild, alongside adults who lead by example."
- **Values:** Build · Belong · Find the way.
- **Values wheel (2026-09-28):** centre **Competence**; four pillars from the mission — **Academics and Technology** (the lab), **Making** (the workshop), **The Outdoors** (the wild), **Mentorship** (adults who lead by example); eight character skills, two per pillar — Curiosity and Critical thinking; Creativity and Initiative; Responsibility and Resilience; Collaboration and Leadership.
- **Partners:** school boards, Children's Services, a university engineering faculty, a parks agency, corporate sponsors.
- **Success measures:** a graduate-to-mentor pipeline, and measured increases in youth wellness.
- **Brand colour:** stone blue-grey with moss-green highlights.
- **Still open:** building character — lodge, workshop or school (revisit before massing).

## Funding model (2026-09-28)
- **Start-up:** Rocky's founding endowment gets the building open and the programs running. It is a starting push, not a permanent subsidy.
- **Sustained support:** ongoing donors, corporate sponsors and grants.
- **The school year pays for the summer.** Sept–June programs are expected to run at a margin: school boards pay per class-week for day camps, weekend camps are booked and paid, and the building can earn from evening and weekend rentals of the gym and meeting room. That surplus subsidizes the **summer and holiday programs**, which are free or sliding-scale for low-income kids.
- **Children's Services** refers kids and sponsors places, especially in the Christmas program.
- **Later option:** after-school care could become another school-year earner if the site is near schools and a licence is worth getting (OQ 12).

## Staff (2026-09-28)
About 11 year-round core staff (executive director, program director, facilities and operations manager, outdoor and expedition coordinator, intake and family liaison, admin support, 3 counsellors, 2 educators), about 2 kitchen staff, and a summer layer of 5 chaperones, about 4 youth workers and 5–6 night staff. **Only the chaperones sleep over**; everyone else works shifts. Full table in `00_Brief.md`.

## Ideas under consideration (2026-09-28, not locked)
- **Waymakers.** Alumni come back at 16–17 as paid junior leaders, and at 18+ as summer staff. It makes the success measure (graduates become mentors) real, gives the excluded 16–17 age band a role without housing it, and adds hands to the summer roster. Plan implication: a small junior-staff room near the pods.
- **Stone Day.** At each summer graduation, every kid adds a stone to a growing cairn in the entry court — 36 a summer. The cohort rotation becomes visible and physical, which could give driver W1 its testable form. A separate public "Build Night" in the gym shows robots and field projects to families and donors, keeping the showcase away from the residential side.
- **The night keeper.** The night staff station is the plan's hinge: one lit room that can see the three pod entries, the sanctuary suite door and the residential entrance. A staffing fact becomes a plan generator, and at night it's the lantern in the elevation.

## Portrait

**The organization.** Cairn Youth Centre is a young nonprofit that would rather be known for what its kids *build* — robots, field projects, a finished expedition — than for the circumstances that brought them there. A founder's endowment opened the doors; the school year keeps them open, with school boards paying for class-week day camps so that summer and holiday places can be free or sliding-scale for kids from low-income families. It runs a lean year-round team that swells each summer, and a deliberately modest footprint (3 pods, 18 beds) that it uses *harder*, through a busy annual calendar, rather than building bigger. Its sharpest tension: the same building has to be a welcoming, public, bookable destination and a private home for 18 kids at a time.

**A typical user's day (summer cohort kid, week 3 of 4).** Wakes in a three-bed room in their pod, breakfast in the shared dining room with the other onsite cohort (the third is out camping this week). Morning in the maker space or the lab with an educator, working toward something they'll show at Build Night. Afternoon: small-group work with the same few adults all week. Evening: pod lounge time, the closest thing to "just being a kid" in the schedule. Bedtime with their pod's chaperone; the night staff come on shift. Next week is graduation, and a stone for the cairn out front.

## Deciders-vs-users gaps

- **Funders and the board want countable outcomes; kids need to not feel counted.** Spaces that serve donor tours or reporting (gym, showcase) stay separate from spaces where a kid is being supported or counselled.
- **Board and funder risk-aversion vs. the camping program's necessary physical risk.** The weather/smoke fallback (basecamp, gym) is the organization's answer to that governance tension, not just a scheduling fix.
- **School boards want a predictable, bounded field-trip product; the mission population needs the building to feel like theirs, not a rented venue.** The school year now carries the finances, so this tension matters more: the same rooms serve both audiences at different times.

## Contradictions worth designing around

1. **Public and bookable (day camps, rentals, donors) vs. private and protected (the residential wing, counselling).** The sharpest contradiction in the brief; it echoes YOUCAN's "welcoming vs. secure" tension, sharper here because the public side now pays the bills.
2. **One front door has to welcome three emotionally distinct arrivals** — celebratory (summer cohort start), routine (day and weekend visitors), homecoming (break-program alumni) — while staff and residential movement use a quieter second entrance.
3. **A building made "harder, not bigger" through scheduling has to make its rotation legible** — one pod empty each summer week, the whole residential wing asleep through the school term. Stone Day is one candidate for making that visible (driver W1).
