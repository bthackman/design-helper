# Client profile (organization) — Cairn Youth Centre (RAIC 400)

**Mode: school/hypothetical.** Every row below is invented, traced to evidence already established in `00_Brief.md` and `02_Decision-Log.md` — not a real organization. **Reframed 2026-09-29 after instructor feedback:** Cairn is now a year-round residential centre for visiting domestic and international students. The social-services profile (low-income and at-risk kids, sanctuary suite, counselling, Children's Services, the school-year-pays-for-summer funding model) is superseded; its history is in `02_Decision-Log.md` and in git.

## Cairn at a glance
- **Name:** Cairn Youth Centre, subtitle "Academics & Outdoors". An academic building with maker spaces and a STEM focus, **not** a public science centre and not an expedition school: academics and making are about two-thirds of the program, the outdoors about one-third. The program also builds **character and confidence**.
- **Founder:** Rocky Pyle (invented, deliberately jokey — a cairn is a rock pile), engineer and entrepreneur. As a kid Rocky spent summers at a mountain camp, where building things and being outdoors first clicked; that led to engineering, then to founding Cairn. His role today (director, or founding board chair with a hired director) is a minor open question (OQ 13).
- **History:** relatively new. Ran as a summer camp in rented space for a few summers; demand from visiting schools and exchange groups led to a year-round building. Summer is still the flagship.
- **Location:** the Calgary region, city or foothills; site still open (OQ 3).
- **Who it serves:** academically inclined, mostly urban students aged 13–16 (grades 8–10, updated 2026-10-05), domestic and international. School classes and exchange groups (up to 32 plus their teachers) come for 12-day sessions from September to June; individually enrolled campers come for 4-week sessions in summer.
- **Mission:** "Cairn builds capable young people, in the lab, the workshop and the wild."
- **Values:** Build · Belong · Find the way.
- **Values wheel:** centre **Competence**; four pillars — **Academics and Technology** (the lab), **Making** (the workshop), **The Outdoors** (the wild), **Mentorship** (adults who lead by example); eight character skills, two per pillar — Curiosity and Critical thinking; Creativity and Initiative; Responsibility and Resilience; Collaboration and Leadership. Unchanged by the reframe. "Belong" now also means belonging across countries: domestic and international kids in the same node.
- **Partners (generic, fictional):** schools and school boards in Western Canada, international exchange and study-abroad programs, a provincial parks agency, a university engineering faculty.
- **Success measures:** sessions where every node completes its trip; the field record growing session by session; schools that rebook; campers who come back.
- **Funding, in one line:** program fees from schools, exchange programs and summer families run it; a founding gift from Rocky Pyle built it.
- **Brand colour:** stone blue-grey with moss-green highlights. Logo: five stacked stones (revised 2026-09-29 to rougher, individual stone shapes).
- **Still open:** building character — lodge, workshop or school (revisit before massing).

## Staff
About 15 year-round (director, program lead, operations and facilities manager, bookings coordinator, 3 educators, expedition lead, 2 field guides, 2–3 kitchen staff, 2 night duty on rota). Visiting teachers (2–4 per class) live in the leader suites during school-year sessions; 4 summer chaperones do the same in July and August. Everyone else works shifts. Full table in `00_Brief.md`.

## Ideas
- **Stone Day (adopted 2026-09-29).** On the last day of every session, each kid adds a stone to a growing cairn in the entry court. About 18 school-year sessions and two summer sessions a year make the rotation visible and physical (driver W1).
- **Field record (adopted 2026-09-29).** Every node adds its trip data (water samples, weather, species counts) to a long-running record of the site shown on the entry-hub wall. It gives the lab work a purpose and connects the indoor two-thirds to the outdoor third (driver W2).
- **Waymakers (idea, lower priority).** Summer campers come back at 16–17 as junior leaders.
- **The night keeper (idea, lower priority).** The night station as the plan's hinge: one lit room that can see the four node entries.

## Portrait

**The organization.** Cairn Youth Centre is a young not-for-profit that brings students from Calgary, the rest of Canada and abroad to the edge of the Rockies for two weeks of building, measuring and camping. It started as a summer camp in rented space; schools and exchange groups kept asking for more, so it built a year-round home. It runs a lean team of about 15 and a deliberately modest building (32 beds, four nodes) that it keeps full through a steady calendar rather than building bigger.

**A typical kid's day (visiting student, day 6 of 12).** Wakes in a four-bed pod in their node; breakfast in the dining room with the other two nodes that are in (the fourth is out on its trip). Morning in the maker space finishing the stream-sampling kit their node takes out on day 8. Afternoon in the lab with an educator, analysing the water samples the last node brought back, and adding them to the field record. Evening in the lounge; bedtime with their teacher in the leader suite next door. On the last day: the showcase, and a stone on the cairn.

## Deciders-vs-users gaps
- **Schools want a predictable, bounded product; kids need the place to feel like theirs for two weeks.** A new group every two weeks can make a building feel like a hotel. The nodes, the lounge and the cairn are what make it feel owned.
- **Risk-aversion vs. the outdoor third's necessary physical risk.** The fallback (basecamp, gym) is the organization's answer, not just a scheduling fix.

## Contradictions worth designing around
1. **Clean arrival vs. muddy return.** Coaches, luggage and first impressions at one door; wet gear, tired kids and showers at the other. The expedition zone is the hinge (driver W3).
2. **Full every night, but never the same kids for more than two weeks.** How does a building with constant turnover still feel like a home? The node scale, Stone Day and the field record are the current answers (drivers W1, W5).
3. **City or wild.** A city site gives the airport, museums, the zoo and the university; a rural site gives the land at the door. Either way the building has to belong to a community (course requirement) and reach the other half by coach.
