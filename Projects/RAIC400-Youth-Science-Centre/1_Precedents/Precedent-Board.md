# Precedent board — RAIC400 Youth Science Centre

*Final, folded-together board — compiled 2026-09-22 from six research campaigns run across three rounds: (1) four parallel typology-baseline campaigns per the signed-off `Search-Plan.md` — youth crisis/residential care, wilderness/outdoor-education, STEM/maker-education, pod/cluster organization; (2) a North America-wide compound-match follow-up (California specifically checked); (3) an international follow-up (Europe, Asia, Australia/Oceania). 42 candidates total, all fetch-verified live before citation. Full query logs (audit trail: campaign/pass/ring/dialect/yield) live in each campaign's original research report — not duplicated here; this board carries the culled, scored, consolidated output.*

## Search frame
- **Typology / scale / climate:** hybrid — youth crisis/residential intervention + wilderness/outdoor-education + STEM/maker-education + multi-modal/variable-occupancy facility. ~24,000 GSF. Calgary, cold-climate.
- **Parti or problem to solve:** how one building holds a public achievement-facing posture and a discreet protected core (W3); how 3 six-bed pods organize (separate volumes / wings-off-spine / shared corridor); how indoor tech-forward space meets outdoor field-science space (W2); how an on-site weather/smoke fallback reads as legitimate, not diminished (W4); how small-group scale survives inside a larger institutional envelope (W5); how a building expresses a genuinely rotating, never-fully-off population (W1).
- **Driver(s):** W1–W5, per `01_Design-Drivers.md` — evaluated per-item, not pre-weighted, per Ben's instruction to prioritize typology over driver-weighting.

## Precedents — full cards (Anchors + standout Lessons)

### P1 · Eva's Phoenix, Toronto — ANCHOR (W1 ● · W3 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** LGA Architectural Partners with ERA Architects, 2017, Toronto ON. Heritage warehouse conversion, 41,200 sf.
- **Source:** [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2017/09/04/lga-architectural-partners-transforms-warehouse-evas-housing-homeless-youth-toronto/)
- **Tags:** youth transitional housing, heritage adaptive reuse, house-cluster organization
- **Why relevant:** The single best built answer in the whole search to W3. Public/achievement-facing shell (heritage brick, classrooms, demonstration kitchen, commercial print shop) wraps a protected residential "neighbourhood" of ten ~5-person townhouse clusters around an internal street. Continuous 24/7 occupancy with rolling turnover toward independent living is a real, if imperfect, analog to W1.
- **Steal:** the internal-street-as-threshold device between public program and residential clusters; townhouse-cluster scale (~5 people) as the human-scale unit.
- **Avoid:** don't over-read the turnover model — Eva's Phoenix residents move toward independent living on individual timelines, not a scheduled weekly rotation like your pods.

### P2 · Wood's Homes — Bowness Campus, Calgary — ANCHOR, thin documentation (W1 ● · W3 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** Hindle Architects (2017 design-development phase; current construction architect unconfirmed), ongoing, Calgary AB. Multi-building, multi-year modernization of a 100-year-old children's mental-health campus.
- **Source:** [Hindle Architects](https://www.hindle-architects.com/woods-homes), [LiveWire Calgary](https://livewirecalgary.com/2026/06/11/province-announces-4m-cash-boost-to-woods-homes-bowness-campus-modernization/)
- **Tags:** local (ring 0), phased campus modernization, continuous-occupancy construction, THIS IS YOUR OWN CITY'S SAME-SECTOR PRECEDENT
- **Why relevant:** Real, current, local proof that ~80 youth can be kept continuously housed through a multi-year phased rebuild — the closest real-world echo of "never fully off" found anywhere across all four campaigns. Organized as discrete small "care centres," not one big building — directly informs the pod-separation question.
- **Steal:** the phasing logic (build new capacity first, then decommission old) as a construction-sequencing lesson; the discrete-small-buildings campus organization.
- **Avoid:** don't cite visual/material details — no plans, renderings, or design narrative beyond press coverage exist for this one. **This is a human-lead precedent, not a drawing precedent** — see Hindle Architects contact below.

### P3 · SOS Children's Village, Djibouti — ANCHOR (W3 ○ · W5 ●) — best plans in the entire set
- **Who/when/where:** Urko Sanchez Architects, 2014, Djibouti (Aga Khan Award nominee 2019). 15 family houses, 10 children each.
- **Source:** [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2019/07/20/sos-childrens-village-urko-sanchez-architects-miniature-walled-city/), [AKDN](https://the.akdn/architecture/project/sos-childrens-village)
- **Tags:** children's residential care, separate-volumes organization, walled compound
- **Why relevant:** The cleanest "separate small volumes" answer for children's residential care at close to your population scale — a walled, medina-like cluster of freestanding houses with narrow connecting streets, not one shared building. Full ground/first-floor plans, sections, and construction detail published — genuinely rare documentation quality for this building family.
- **Steal:** independent-house-plus-shared-central-services logic; "organic surveillance" (staff oversight without institutional feel) via compound layout, not corridors.
- **Avoid:** hot-desert climate — use for organization only, not envelope/building-physics lessons.

### P4 · SOS Children's Village, Aqaba, Jordan — LESSON, bordering Anchor (W3 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** Jafar Tukan & Partners, 1991, Aqaba (Aga Khan Award winner, 1999–2001 cycle). 20,000 m², nine family houses.
- **Source:** [Aga Khan Trust for Culture](https://the.akdn/en/how-we-work/our-agencies/aga-khan-trust-culture/akaa/sos-childrens-village)
- **Tags:** campus-scale organization, admin/residential separation, courtyard threshold
- **Why relevant:** A different SOS Village, independently surfaced by the crisis-residential campaign (not the pod-organization campaign) — the convergence with P3 above, from two unrelated search angles, is itself the finding: **"several small houses + one separate shared admin/program building, linked by courtyard thresholds" appears to be close to a typology-independent organizational law for this building family.** Directly maps to your "3 pods + shared hall" program.
- **Steal:** courtyard/archway as the literal threshold device between the protected residential cluster and the public/institutional program cluster (admin, kindergarten, sports hall — your STEM labs/gym analog).
- **Avoid:** hot-climate materials (granite infill, shading strategy) — organization only.

### P5 · MAKESHOP + MuseumLAB, Children's Museum of Pittsburgh — ANCHOR (W2 ● · W3 ○ · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** Koning Eizenberg Architecture. MAKESHOP ~2011; MuseumLAB (teen wing, age 10+, closest age match of any candidate found) 2019, adaptive reuse of an 1890 Carnegie library.
- **Source:** [Koning Eizenberg](https://www.kearch.com/childrens-museum-of-pittsburgh), [Pittsburgh Magazine](https://www.pittsburghmagazine.com/musuemlab-a-finalist-for-its-innovative-design/)
- **Tags:** maker space, teen-specific, adaptive reuse, "beautiful ruin"
- **Why relevant:** Best single answer to keeping real fabrication space (wood/metal/circuitry) from reading as industrial or institutional — explicit design brief was "unpolished, accessible, functional," exposed original structure "to tell the story of how things are made." Closest age-population match of anything found across all 32 candidates.
- **Steal:** the "expose structure as pedagogy, not as cost-cutting" principle — a design *decision*, stated explicitly in your narrative, not an accident.
- **Avoid:** whole-building scale mismatch (47,000 sf historic library) — use for the maker-space component only.

### P6 · Chewonki Foundation Campus, Maine — LESSON, strongest single hit on W4
- **Who/when/where:** Center for Environmental Education: Theodore + Theodore Architects, 1999. Girls Camp cabins: OPAL Architecture, 2017 (Phase 1 of master plan). Wiscasset, Maine.
- **Source:** [OPAL Architecture](https://www.opalarch.us/chewonki/), [Theodore + Theodore](https://www.2tarch.com/?portfolio=chewonki-center-for-environmental-education), [Chewonki](https://chewonki.org/community/chewonki-outdoor-classroom/overnight-field-trips/environmental-education-base-camp-experience/)
- **Tags:** weather resilience, "base camp experience," shed-roof cabins
- **Why relevant:** The single sharpest built answer to W4's central question found anywhere in the search. OPAL's own design notes state the shed-roof cabin forms exist specifically "to permit brighter, more spacious interiors to better accommodate indoor activities during rainy weather" — resilience-for-bad-weather is designed into the *primary* sleeping structure from day one, not a separate, differently-branded backup room.
- **Steal:** generosity of section/light in the primary structure as the resilience strategy — argues against a literal separate "storm shelter" and for making your flexible hall generous enough that it reads as a real program space regardless of whether camping happened that week.
- **Avoid:** none significant — well-documented, directly transferable.

### P7 · Nueva School Science and Environmental Center, Hillsborough CA — LESSON, strongest single hit on W2
- **Who/when/where:** Leddy Maytum Stacy Architects, 2021. 11,600 sf, 8 science classrooms.
- **Source:** [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/15990-leddy-maytum-stacys-science-and-environmental-center-embraces-its-landscape-with-sustainable-goals)
- **Tags:** indoor/outdoor hinge, sliding walls, field-science integration
- **Why relevant:** Clearest literal built version of W2's indoor-lab/outdoor-field-science hinge found in the entire search — one-room-deep classroom bar, broad sliding doors let lab activity physically extend into surrounding woodland rather than merely adjoining it. Explicitly "doesn't hit you over the head with its technological feats" (Architectural Record's words) — restraint as a design principle.
- **Steal:** sliding walls turning lab space into outdoor field-science space on demand; cable reels instead of fixed wiring to avoid an industrial-infrastructure read.
- **Avoid:** none significant.

### P8 · Samuel House, Shawnigan Lake School, BC — LESSON, most literal "3 pods → 1 building" precedent
- **Who/when/where:** Architect unconfirmed (do not attribute to Christine Lintott Architects — their other Shawnigan work was confirmed, this specific building's authorship was not). Opened 2022–24. Canada's first co-ed Grade-8 boarding house.
- **Source:** [Shawnigan Lake School](https://www.shawnigan.ca/news-detail?pk=1352619)
- **Tags:** wings-off-spine, house merger, identity preservation
- **Why relevant:** The most literal real-world test of your exact open question — two formerly-separate small residential houses merged into two wings of one building around a shared central "gathering" zone (cook/eat/socialize/study). The trade-off is made explicit in the school's own account: cohesion and shared-staffing efficiency gained, at the cost of each house's stand-alone identity, retained only through color/heraldry rather than separate volumes.
- **Steal:** identity-preservation-through-color/orientation-gesture as the mechanism when you choose the wings-off-spine option over separate volumes.
- **Avoid:** no plans found — narrative/photos only; and it's fixed-cohort-per-year, not evidence for a rotating population (W1).

### P9 · Yale — Benjamin Franklin & Pauli Murray Colleges — ANCHOR, W5
- **Who/when/where:** Robert A.M. Stern Architects, 2017, New Haven CT. Two residential colleges, 452 students each.
- **Source:** [RAMSA](https://www.ramsa.com/news/article/yale-university-dedicates-benjamin-franklin-college-and-pauli-murray-college), [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/design/robert-a-m-stern-on-the-design-of-the-new-pauli-murray-and-benjamin-franklin-colleges_o/)
- **Tags:** entryway system, wings-off-courtyard
- **Why relevant:** Yale's historic "entryway" system is the cleanest documented version of the wings-off-a-shared-spine strategy — except the spine is an outdoor courtyard, not an interior corridor. Each entryway (historically ~20–30 students) is genuinely self-contained circulation-wise while sharing a common ground plane — this scales down more naturally to 6-bed-pod size than Campus North's hub model does.
- **Steal:** entryway-as-independent-stair-cluster logic, scaled down.
- **Avoid:** full plans not independently confirmed — treat as diagram-level evidence.

### P10 · Campus North Residential Commons, University of Chicago — ANCHOR, W5
- **Who/when/where:** Studio Gang, 2016, Chicago IL. 800 beds in eight "Houses" of ~100 students, distributed through three linked towers.
- **Source:** [Studio Gang](https://studiogang.com/projects/university-of-chicago-campus-north-residential-commons/), [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/799351/university-of-chicago-campus-north-residential-commons-studio-gang)
- **Tags:** communal hub per cluster, stacked-within-tower
- **Why relevant:** A third organizational option beyond stacked/wings/corridor — a dedicated multi-story communal hub per cluster, threaded through shared building fabric. Identity comes from the hub, not the envelope.
- **Steal:** the "hub, not envelope, carries identity" principle — most useful if your pods end up sharing a building fabric and need identity from within rather than from massing.
- **Avoid:** scale mismatch (100 people/House vs. your 6/pod) — the hub-per-cluster logic needs real down-scaling, and full plans weren't confirmed viewable.

## C7 — Compound-match search (broadened beyond Calgary/Alberta, added 2026-09-22 per Ben's request)

**The question:** does a real building/program combine all three threads — crisis-care mission, STEM/academic method, wilderness-expedition component — closer to matching this project's actual hybrid, rather than requiring synthesis across the four separate sub-typologies above? Searched California first (Ben's explicit interest), then broadened nationally/internationally.

**Bottom line: no single full 3-thread match exists** — confirms the C5a–d finding rather than reversing it. But two real, well-documented institutions each hit 2 of 3 threads strongly, with **complementary missing thirds** — together they're a closer approximation of the brief than any single prior precedent.

### P11 · Pine River Institute, near Shelburne, Ontario — ANCHOR for crisis-care + wilderness (W2 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** No architect of record found (institutional client-side project). Campus 2014; Moffat House dormitory 2021; Lang House 2022; Neal Hall 2023; Outdoor Leadership Experience base 2023.

### P14 · Oasis Restore (secure school), Kent, UK — ANCHOR, tightest 2-of-3 match found anywhere (W2 ● · W3 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** No named design architect found (delivery: Galliford Try / MultiCAD). Opened 2024, £26M, Rochester/Medway. UK's first "secure school" under the 2016 Taylor Review — 49 beds, ages 12–18.
- **Source:** [GOV.UK feature](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/inside-the-oasis-restore-secure-school), [Oasis Restore](https://www.oasisrestore.org/), [MultiCAD](https://multicad.co.uk/projects/oasis-restore-secure-school-kent/)

### P15 · Egely (secure youth institution), Nørre Åby, Denmark — best pod-scale + crisis-sanctuary precedent in the ENTIRE search (W1 ○ · W3 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** KHR Arkitekter, competition won 2004, ~2,700 m². 20 beds, ages 15–17.
- **Source:** [KHR Arkitekter](https://khr.dk/projekter/egely/), [Socialcenter Lillebælt](https://socialcenterlillebaelt.dk/afdelinger/egely/om-egely)
- **Why relevant:** **This is the single closest built analog to your exact program structure found across all five research campaigns:** four independent 5-resident pods (not one dorm mass) — remarkably close to your 3 pods of 6 — **one of which is a separately-secured unit for youth in extreme crisis**, functioning almost exactly like your 1–2 bed sanctuary suite, just embedded in a custodial rather than voluntary program. This is the strongest available evidence that your sanctuary-suite-separate-from-the-main-pods decision is architecturally sound.
- **Steal:** four-pod-plus-one-specially-secured-unit as a direct structural precedent for "3 pods + 1 sanctuary suite."
- **Avoid:** secure/custodial context — soften the security language when adapting; documentation is moderate (no full plan set), a Region Syddanmark benchmark PDF exists but wasn't machine-readable.

### P16 · Children's Home of the Future, Kerteminde, Denmark — LESSON, best-documented single building in the whole search, strongest W3 precedent
- **Who/when/where:** CEBRA, 2014, ~1,500 m². 24-hour care for behavioral/social/mental-health needs.
- **Source:** [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2014/11/21/childrens-home-of-the-future-cebra-denmark-fragmented-gables-extruded-windows/), [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/570664/children-s-home-cebra)
- **Why relevant:** Four abstracted gabled "house" volumes in domestic materials (clay tile, wood siding) so the building reads as a residential cluster, not an institution — entrance deliberately set back from the street to reduce visibility/stigma, wings age-zoned (younger toward garden, teens toward street). The entire design concept is literally about disguising an institutional program as an archetypal "home" — the best-documented W3 precedent found anywhere (full plans, photos, multiple independent sources).
- **Steal:** the "disguise as archetypal home silhouette" strategy directly; age-zoned wings with dormer "bonus spaces" for individual retreat.
- **Avoid:** none significant — best documentation in the set, safe to cite directly.

### P17 · Timbertop, Geelong Grammar School, Victoria, Australia — best W4 precedent in the ENTIRE search (W4 ● · W5 ●)
- **Who/when/where:** School-built (students helped construct original 1950s units); John Lewis Centre bunker 2011, architect not identified. Est. 1953. 325-hectare Year 9 wilderness campus, ~240 fourteen-year-olds, 16 units of 14–16 students.
- **Source:** [Geelong Grammar](https://ggs.vic.edu.au/learning/campuses/year-9-at-timbertop/), [Wikipedia, corroborating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbertop)
- **Why relevant:** The real, 70+-year-running original of the "wilderness year" model — 50–55 nights of camping/hiking culminating in a 33km marathon. **The John Lewis Centre is the single strongest W4 precedent found across every campaign:** a semi-underground bunker of three ordinary daily-use classrooms that combine into one bushfire refuge-of-last-resort, built after real 2006/2009 evacuation events — this is literally your flexible-hall-as-resilience-space idea, already built, already proven in an actual wildfire-adjacent climate.
- **Steal:** the "everyday classroom doubles as emergency refuge, no separate labeled shelter" principle, directly. 14–16-student unit scale for W5.
- **Avoid:** thinner than its reputation suggests architecturally — no plans, no named architect for the units or the bunker itself, no elite private-school population match (not a crisis-care precedent). Fame is programmatic, not architectural — treat as a principle to steal, not a building to cite drawings from.

## Lessons — cross-campaign synthesis

*(Distilled across all six campaigns — not a repeat of each campaign's own list, which is preserved in the original research reports.)*

1. **The public/secure duality is almost never solved by a literal wing split — it's solved by re-coding what "secure" looks like.** Sightlines instead of locked doors, a corridor that reads as a street (Sanderson), a cottage that reads as a home not a barracks (Wood's Homes), a heritage-warehouse shell wrapping a residential "neighbourhood" (Eva's Phoenix). W3's answer is almost always a materials/sightline move, not a plan-diagram move.

2. **"Several small houses + one separate shared admin/program building, linked by a courtyard/threshold" independently recurred across three unrelated campaigns** (SOS Villages ×2, Wood's Homes Bowness, and echoed in Eva's Phoenix's public-shell-wraps-residential-cluster logic). This is close to a typology-independent organizational law for this building family — the strongest single piece of evidence for how your 3 pods + shared hall could organize.

3. **Three real pod/cluster strategies exist with genuine, legible trade-offs** — separate volumes (maximum identity/turnover independence, less shared-infrastructure efficiency), wings/entries off a shared spine (Yale, Samuel House — the best-documented middle path at your actual 2–3-cluster scale), and shared corridor with a real communal hub per cluster (Campus North — efficient, but Simmons Hall proves a hub or genuine spatial anchor is required, color-coding alone fails).

4. **The best weather/risk-resilience answer is generosity built into the primary structure, not a separate branded backup room** (Chewonki). This directly argues for making your flexible hall generous in section/light on its own terms, not a diminished "storm shelter."

5. **Achievement-forward identity works best as ambient/continuous display woven into primary circulation, not an isolated trophy room** (High Tech High's Main Street gallery) — directly relevant to your flexible hall's exhibition/science-fair function.

6. **Small-group scale earns *measurable* behavioral returns at the grain of privacy** (bunk dividers, personal lockers, sightlines — Arroyo Village/Laradon), not just at the massing/room-count level. Worth citing as the "prove it works" argument in your Design Brief, not just an aesthetic preference.

7. **W2 (method follows setting) has its strongest, most literal answer in Nueva's sliding-wall classroom-to-landscape hinge**, and its structural/tectonic-metaphor answer in Bamfield's timber shells — two genuinely different, both transferable, ways to express the same driver.

## Honest gap — W1 ("never fully off")

**Still the weakest-evidenced driver overall, but the international round narrowed it.** No precedent anywhere — across all six research campaigns, five countries, three continents — documents a building architecturally supporting a genuinely *rotating/cycling* population on your exact staggered 4-week schedule. But **Egely (Denmark, P15)** is now the closest real echo found: a specially-secured crisis unit permanently embedded within an otherwise-stable 4-pod system is structurally similar to your sanctuary-suite-within-3-pods logic, even though it's not a scheduled rotation. Combined with Wood's Homes Bowness (continuous-occupancy-through-construction) and Eva's Phoenix (rolling turnover), W1 has moved from "zero support" to "no direct match, but three adjacent structural analogs to build an argument from."

**Still worth reframing as a strength, not a failure.** Per the course rubric's Extensions column ("synthesizes architectural ideas that extend notions of modularity and geometry beyond a collection of units"), a design driver with no direct precedent is exactly the kind of original contribution that column rewards — but it means W1 needs to be argued from first principles and the operational scheduling logic already locked in `00_Brief.md`, using Egely/Wood's Homes/Eva's Phoenix as structural analogs, not literal matches. Flag this explicitly in the Design Brief.

**W4 moved the opposite direction — from "one strong hit" to "two of the best hits in the whole search."** Chewonki's built-in-generosity answer now sits alongside Timbertop's John Lewis Centre (P17) — a real bushfire bunker that IS the daily classrooms, not a separate labeled shelter. Between them, W4 is now one of the best-supported drivers on the board, not a thin one.
