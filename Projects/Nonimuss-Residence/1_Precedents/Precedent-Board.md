## C6 technical findings — pool-room envelope rules (Evidence, feeds massing + materiality + OQ-6)
Ten sourced concept-grade rules; headline set: (1) continuous AIR barrier beats vapour-barrier perfectionism — airflow is the killer at -30°C; (2) wood-frame answer = ALL insulation continuous outside the structure (GBA/Holladay; BSC BSI-055 "In the Deep End" is the canonical read — next fetch if authorized); (3) warm-side sealed vapour retarder where used; (4) pool room at negative pressure, vestibuled from the house; (5) 50–60% RH, dedicated dehumidification, air temp ≥ water temp, pool cover; (6) triple glazing + thermally broken frames AND active air-wash/drained sills — passive alone won't hold the glass dry at design cold; (7) chloramine air = SCC risk: no standard stainless structure in the humid zone; (8) every finish/fastener specced for permanent 60% RH; (9) Alberta ring-1 lesson: Poole's retrofit bill = pool heat + glazing; Love's = envelope (7 furnaces); (10) blower-door the pool room while accessible. Full sourced list in campaign report; sources incl. Construction Specifier (SGH), GreenBuildingAdvisor, PoolPak, DCA, AQUA.

## Precedents (cards)

### P1 · Poole Residence — ANCHOR (D1 ● · D2 ● · D3 ●)
- **Who / when / where:** Don Bittorf & James Wensley, 1968, Westridge Park, Edmonton — West Coast Modernism. Ring 1. Visit: drive-by.
- **Source:** [Globe & Mail — "green update"](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/real-estate/calgary-and-edmonton/edmontons-modernist-landmark-poole-house-gets-a-greenupdate/article36146487/) · [Effect Homes retrofit](https://www.effecthomes.ca/post/iconic-modern-home-designs-with-sustainable-renovations) · [Edmonton Historical Board — Bittorf & Wensley](https://www.edmontonhistoricalboard.com/organizations/bittorf-and-wensley-architects/)
- **Tags:** indoor-pool · woodland-ravine · cedar+rock · eco-retrofit · Alberta
- **Why relevant:** the existence proof — a *full-sized indoor pool* living as an ordinary part of an Alberta family house set in 6 ha of woodland over a ravine. Our closest climate + program analogue.
- **Steal:** pool as an integral, unfussy room; cedar-and-rock palette married to a wooded slope; the retrofit lesson that the pool's running cost is *heat + glazing*, solvable with solar-thermal (they added an array just to heat the pool).
- **Avoid:** it predates our 120 m² cap problem — Poole doesn't argue the pool out of a floor-area budget, so don't cite it *as* the cap argument (that's OQ-6, still ours to make).

### P2 · Cedarvale Ravine House — ANCHOR (D1 ● · D2 ● · D4 ○)
- **Who / when / where:** Drew Mandel Architects, 2012, Toronto (mid-town ravine edge). ~3,350 ft² (2.6× our cap — scale caveat). Full drawings (plans + 2 sections).
- **Source:** [Drew Mandel Architects](https://drewmandelarchitects.com/cedarvale-ravine-house/) · [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2013/02/15/cedarvale-ravine-house-in-toronto-by-drew-mandel-architects/) · [Archello](https://archello.com/project/cedarvale-ravine-house)
- **Tags:** suburban-front / forest-rear · lap-pool · stone+zinc+walnut · cantilever
- **Why relevant:** the one card that teaches the *suburban lot with a wild rear* condition we actually have (answers OQ-14). Dignified street face; the plan weaves toward a glass-enclosed social room at the forest edge; cantilever hovers over the pool.
- **Steal:** the promenade from a reticent street face to an all-glass social room at the rear wall; graded material story (heavy stone at street → light glass at forest); pool tucked under a cantilever.
- **Avoid:** the budget and floor area are estate-scale — take the *organization*, not the square footage or the three-stone masonry program.

### P3 · House of Trough — ANCHOR (D3 ● · D4 ● · D5 ● at 98 m²!)
- **Who / when / where:** Jun Igarashi Architects, 2008, east Hokkaido, Japan. ~98 m². Full drawings (plans, elevations, sectional models).
- **Source:** [Architonic](https://www.architonic.com/en/project/jun-igarashi-architects-house-of-trough/5100776) · [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/jun-igarashi-architects-house-of-trough/) · [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/8518-house-of-trough)
- **Tags:** buffer-zone-section · clerestory / high-side-light · compact cold-climate · windowless-core-daylit
- **Why relevant:** the section our winter demands. A square plan sandwiches living between service/storage (N) and bedrooms/storage (S) buffer bands; the core is shielded from view yet daylit from high side-light bounced in — and it does it at *98 m²*, inside our cap. Directly answers the site finding that the rear (forest) face gets no direct winter sun.
- **Steal:** the buffer-zone parti — wrap the conditioned core in storage/service, light it from above/high-side, not from a cold glazed wall; internal eave that cuts summer sun but passes winter sun.
- **Avoid:** the deliberately view-denying exterior (their site was an iron factory) — we *want* the forest view on one face, so borrow the section, invert the opacity toward the Weaselhead.

### P5 · Cabin Vindheim — LESSON (D3 ● · D4 ● · D5 ● at 55 m²)

- **Source:** [Vardehaugen](https://vardehaugen.no/projects/built/cabin-vindheim) · [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/vardehaugen-cabin-vindheim-house-lillehammer-norway-04-19-2016/) · [Wallpaper*](https://www.wallpaper.com/architecture/vindheim-cabin-by-vardehaugen-is-a-hidden-wonder)

## Lessons — the transferable moves (the actual output)

**L1 · Buffer-zone section beats a glass rear wall.** *(House of Trough, Ravine Guest House, Six Acres → D3, D4, D5)* Wrap the conditioned core in service/storage/bedroom bands and daylight it from high side-light or a clerestory, not from a cold glazed wall. This is the direct answer to the site finding that the rear/forest face gets **no direct winter sun** — stop trying to light the plan from the shaded forest edge; light it from above and from the buffer.

**L2 · Address the forest on one face, and make the threshold a band, not a line.** *(Cedarvale, Fahouse, Six Acres → D1)* Put private/service rooms to the street/entry; open the social rooms to the forest wall. Two valid readings to test in massing: a single all-glass social room (Cedarvale) vs. discrete framed rooms each metering a piece of forest (Fahouse). The threshold is a deck/court band you occupy, not a pane you look through.

**L3 · Let the pool be its own volume you *pass into*, and use an operable wall to make it four-season.** *(Poole, Integral, Cedarvale, RGH → D2, OQ-6, OQ-15, materiality)* The humidity envelope wants to be discrete — attached-but-vestibuled, a link, or a pavilion. The "one wind-sheltered ritual" happens through an *opening threshold* (Integral's sliding wall, RGH's operable fire-glass), not through more floor area — which is also how the pool stays outside the 120 m² cap argument.

**L4 · The cap is the discipline, not a hardship — compact one-orientation plans work in snow country.** *(House of Trough 98 m², Cabin Vindheim 55 m², Six Acres → D5)* Three built proofs that step-free, corridor-light plans aimed one way survive real winters. Reframes D5 from constraint to parti.

**L5 · When grade is snow and shade, daylight comes from the roof.** *(Cabin Vindheim, Strathmore, RGH clerestory → D4, D2)* Glaze the roof plane / clerestory to get sky-light onto the pool and into mid-plan when the low winter sun is blocked by the forest wall and the south neighbour. Pairs with L1.

**L6 · Envelope is the whole game here, and it's a solvable line item.** *(Poole retrofit, Love "7 furnaces", Sask. Conservation House, C6 rules → D3, materiality, OQ-6)* Every local precedent that touched a pool learned the cost is heat + glazing + air-barrier — and every one solved it (Poole's solar-thermal array). Design the pool-room envelope to C6 rules from day one; don't inherit 1957–69 performance with the 1957–69 planning.

- **D2 wellness-sequence-as-event — STRONG.** P1 (pool as ordinary room), P7 (operable wall = four-season), P4 (hearth stitch), P2 (pool under cantilever). Mechanism + feeling both covered.
- **D3 winter-is-the-design-case — STRONG.** P1, P3, P5, P8 + the Sask. Conservation counter-example. Well covered.
- **D4 sun-is-designed — ADEQUATE, now honest.** Was the thin driver; Six Acres (built Alberta, clerestory, ~53°N) plus Hindle (Ring-0 stepped-for-light) close the "no local D4 example" gap. Section teachers (P3 Japan, P5 Norway) are latitude-foreign — see Q3.
- **D5 step-free-zero-corridor — WEAK FIT (named).** The set proves *compactness* (55/98 m²) but **no carded precedent actually demonstrates a zero-corridor, step-free adjacency plan.** Compact ≠ corridor-free ≠ wheelchair-turnable. This driver is under-supported by precedent → OQ-16.

**The 3 hardest questions a review would ask:**
1. **The load-bearing move has zero precedent support.** Every pool precedent either predates the 120 m² cap (Poole) or is estate-scale and ignores it (Integral, Cedarvale, RGH). Arguing a *conditioned* pool volume out of "developed floor space" (OQ-6) rests on bylaw definition, not on any house shown here. If the instructor rejects the reading, the whole parti moves. Draft that argument before massing.
2. **Does compactness masquerade as D5?** Trough and Vindheim are small, but small isn't step-free-and-corridor-free. We have no plan precedent for the actual D5 test (0 m² dedicated corridor; guest+bath+living core on one step-free level). Need a space-planning-phase precedent or an explicit test-fit study.
3. **Winter-sun geometry doesn't transfer.** The two best section teachers sit at ~43°N (Hokkaido) and ~61°N (Lillehammer); Calgary is ~51°N with a 15.6° max winter altitude and a forest wall + south neighbour already shown to kill direct rear winter sun. Borrow the buffer-zone/roof-glazing *logic*, but re-run the sun angles locally — don't copy their aperture sizes.
