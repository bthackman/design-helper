# Material palette — Nonimuss Residence

*Phase 5 opened 2026-08-28 — first real run of the drafted candidate-palette protocol. Basis:
Option B "The Breezeway" (LOCKED, massing) and the reconciled ground-floor program (space
planning). Read against locked drivers D1–D5, precedent lessons L1–L6, and the site's sun/wind
findings.*

## Frame
- **Climate/durability constraints from the site:** -20 to -30°C cold-outbreak wind from N–NE
  (harshest exposure face); prevailing W–SW wind including Chinook gusts >80 km/h arriving across
  the open Weaselhead/reservoir gap with little upwind shelter — hits the rear wellness terrace and
  the pool/link's operable wall directly. Winter-noon sun reliable only from the E and high-S; the
  rear/forest face gets **no direct winter sun at all** (Site-Details sun study). Freeze-thaw is a
  real annual cycle, not an edge case.
- **Budget tier (from the brief):** client profile row 12 — "splurge: envelope, wellness cluster,
  kitchen; economize: area, complexity, finishes-as-status." Quality-per-m² is the game, not
  quality-per-dollar spread evenly. The wellness/pool sequence is explicitly where the flair budget
  concentrates (D2's own test line): "restraint everywhere else."
- **Material lessons from the precedent board:** L1 (buffer-zone section beats a glazed rear wall —
  daylight the forest-facing rooms from above/the side, not through glass aimed at the shaded
  forest); L2 (address the forest on one face, make the threshold a band); L3 (the pool is its own
  volume you *pass into*, made four-season by an operable wall — not more floor area); L5 (when
  grade is snow/shade, daylight comes from the roof); L6 (the pool room's envelope is a solvable
  line item if designed to C6 rules from day one, the way Poole's solar-thermal retrofit did).
- **Driver(s) a palette should serve:** all five, but D2 and D3 are load-bearing here — the
  wellness sequence is the one thing this project is willing to spend on, and winter is the design
  case the envelope has to survive, not just meet code for.

## Candidate palettes
Swept `Library/Materials/{Envelope,Structure,Interior}.md` (seeded 2026-08-28, drafted/unverified)
pooled with fresh consideration of what this specific site and program actually need. All three
candidates draw from the same library — the difference between them is the argument each one
makes, not which materials exist.

### Palette A — "Heavy hearth, light forest skin"
- **Envelope:** exterior mineral wool continuous insulation + standing-seam steel at the pool
  pavilion's forest-facing roof/wall (sheds snow, no freeze-thaw risk, cheapest way to hit L6's C6
  target) — L6, D3 — $$ — high durability, moderate carbon. Triple-glazed fibreglass windows and
  the operable threshold wall at the link (L3, OQ-15) — $$$ — non-negotiable at this latitude.
- **Structure:** wood light-frame at the house (D5's compactness, local trades) — $ — low carbon.
  Exposed glulam at the pool hall's high roof, carrying L5's roof-glazing/clerestory move so
  daylight reaches the water without a glazed forest-facing wall (L1) — $$ — moderate carbon,
  earns its cost by doing L1 *and* L5's work at once.
- **Interior:** board-formed concrete floor and hearth-wall at the house-to-pool threshold — the
  thermal-mass anchor for D2's ritual and a genuinely durable wet-adjacent surface — L3, D2 — $$$
  — high embodied carbon, the one place that's honestly justified by driver work rather than taste.
  White oak flooring/millwork in the house's daylit rooms (client taste: "texture, light,
  acoustics over gesture") — $$–$$$.
- **Driver scorecard:** D1 ○ · D2 ✓ · D3 ✓ · D4 ○ · D5 ~ (material choice is roughly orthogonal to
  plan organization; doesn't help or hurt)
- **Survives value engineering?** Board-formed concrete is the single most expensive line item.
  Cutting it to polished (not board-formed) concrete keeps the thermal mass, the wet-zone
  durability, and most of the "heavy hearth" argument — weaker as a *statement*, but the argument
  survives. **Yes, with a visible but not fatal loss.**
- **Status:** active

### Palette B — "One material, honestly aged"
- **Envelope:** cedar rainscreen (D1, L2) everywhere except the pool room's high-humidity interior
  face, where fibre-cement lap siding substitutes on the exterior only — cedar can't take sustained
  interior humidity even with a rainscreen gap — $$–$$$. Same triple-glazed operable wall at the
  link as Palette A (L3, OQ-15) — $$$.
- **Structure:** wood light-frame throughout, kept simple and cheap on purpose — D5, local trades —
  $ — the saved budget is deliberately banked for the operable-threshold hardware OQ-15 still needs
  specified (barn-track / greenhouse-vent / storm-shutter mechanism, not yet solved at any phase).
- **Interior:** cedar continues *inside* at the link and the great room ceiling — the same material
  crossing the threshold in both directions, which is the whole argument: the boundary between
  "outside, forest" and "inside, wellness ritual" (D2, L3) dissolves rather than gets detailed away.
  Polished concrete at the pool/entry wet zone (practical, pairs with radiant floor heat) — $–$$.
- **Driver scorecard:** D1 ✓✓ · D2 ✓✓ · D3 ○ (envelope performance rides on the mineral wool behind
  the cedar, not on the cedar itself — real, just doing less visible work than Palette A's mass)
  · D4 ~ · D5 ~
- **Survives value engineering?** **No — and this is the honest finding, not a footnote.** The
  entire argument is that one material crosses the threshold. Substituting fibre-cement anywhere
  beyond the pool room's forced exception breaks the continuity the palette is *named for*. Cedar
  here is doing 100% driver work, not taste — which is exactly what the gate question is supposed
  to catch, and exactly why this is the strongest D1/D2 case of the three candidates and the most
  fragile one under budget pressure.
- **Status:** active

### Palette C — "Budget envelope, one splurge room" (named fallback)
- **Envelope:** fibre-cement lap siding + brick veneer at the base, steel standing-seam roof — the
  cheapest proven cold-climate assembly, no site-specific argument attached. Full triple-glazed
  operable wall + exterior mineral wool concentrated at the pool room only, matching D2's own test
  line ("flair budget concentrated here, restraint everywhere else") almost literally.
- **Structure:** wood light-frame throughout — $ — cheapest option available.
- **Interior:** painted drywall + polished concrete in the house (budget); white oak + a wood
  ceiling reserved for the pool/wellness room only, where the spend is concentrated.
- **Driver scorecard:** D1 ✗ (the house itself makes no forest-responsive material argument — the
  house material language would be indistinguishable from an unrelated suburban lot) · D2 ✓
  (matches the driver's own test line) · D3 ○ · D4 ~ · D5 ~
- **Survives value engineering?** There's almost nothing left to cut — the "splurge" is already the
  one thing D2 and C6 (L6) require regardless of budget, so this palette is already sitting at the
  floor. **Trivially "survives," but only because it never spent anything to lose.**
- **Status:** parked — safest on budget, but fails D1 outright, which the massing and precedent
  phases both treated as load-bearing (the whole parti borrows the Weaselhead). Kept on record as
  the fallback if a real budget check later forces retreat from A or B.

## Phase gate — skeptic pass
**If value engineering cuts 10%, which material goes first and does the palette survive it?**
Answered per candidate above — A survives with a visible loss (concrete finish downgrade), B does
not survive without breaking its own argument, C was never spending enough to have this problem.

**Which material is doing driver work, and which is just taste?** In A, the board-formed concrete
is driver work (D2 thermal anchor); the white oak is closer to taste, defensible but not
load-bearing. In B, *everything* is driver work — that's the palette's whole appeal and its whole
risk. In C, only the pool room's materials are driver work; the rest of the house is unargued.

**What will this look like after ten winters?** A ages predictably (concrete doesn't change,
metal roofing weathers slowly). B ages *visibly and on purpose* — cedar silvers — which is either
the honest answer to this exact question or a maintenance conversation the client hasn't had yet
(re-oil vs. let it silver is a real decision, not a detail). C ages fine because it was never
asked to do anything distinctive.

**Read against massing and site:** the massing lock (Option B "Breezeway") already made the
material argument partway — an operable, insulated link as the wellness threshold. Both A and B
extend that logic; C doesn't engage it. Neither A nor B loops back to phase 3 geometry — this
reads as confirmation of Option B's parti, not a contradiction of it.

**Would the Studio Critic voice change this?** Not run this session. The likely question if it
were: cedar interior at a pool-adjacent ceiling (Palette B's great-room-ceiling move) needs a
vented assembly per the humidity note already flagged in `Library/Materials/Interior.md` — worth
a real check before locking B's interior scope, not just noting it here.

## Locked palette

**DECISION 2026-08-28: Palette B "One material, honestly aged" LOCKED**, with Palette A's
fallback explicitly carried rather than discarded.

**The trade, stated explicitly:** B is the *best* argument for D1 and D2 — the two drivers this
project is actually willing to spend its "flair budget" on — at the cost of being the least
resilient to a real budget cut. That's the same shape of trade this project already made once, at
the massing lock (Option B "Breezeway" over the safer Option C, because D2 was worth the extra
envelope to heat). Consistency with that precedent, not just the material argument on its own
merits, is part of why B wins here over the safer Palette A.

**Named mitigation, not a discard-if-cut plan:** if a real budget check later forces a cut, the
fallback is *not* "switch to Palette C." It's to confine any fibre-cement substitution to the
single least-visible face (the N wall, which is already the harshest-exposure, least-daylit face
per the wind study) and keep the cedar at the link, the great room ceiling, and every
forest-facing wall — the threshold-crossing move survives even if total cedar area shrinks. This
preserves the argument the same way Palette A's concrete downgrade preserves *its* argument; B
just needed the mitigation named explicitly, since "the whole palette" was too coarse a unit to
either keep or cut.

**Carried open questions:** OQ-15 (operable threshold hardware at local-trades budget) is now
squarely this phase's to close, not massing's — see Flags below. The Studio Critic's vented-ceiling
question above should be answered before this locks into a construction document, not before this
phase gate; noting it here is the honest way to not let it get lost.

## Flags
- **OQ-15 (operable threshold hardware) is now this phase's to close.** Palette B's whole argument
  depends on the link's operable wall actually working at local-trades budget and in W–SW Chinook
  gusts over 80 km/h — a barn-track, greenhouse-vent, or storm-shutter mechanism needs to be
  specified before this locks into a construction document, not left as "proven by Integral House"
  (which used a different budget and a different wind exposure entirely).
- **Cedar ceiling near the pool hall needs a vented-assembly check** — carried from the phase-gate
  skeptic pass above. Unresolved; copy to `03_Open-Questions.md` if not closed before space
  planning finalizes the pool-hall roof assembly.
- **Cedar maintenance posture (re-oil vs. natural silvering) is an unmade client decision**, not a
  materiality-phase call — surface it at the next client conversation rather than defaulting one
  way in the drawings.
- **First-run finding on the protocol itself:** the new phase-5 reference's "promote back to the
  library" step assumes a real build confirms the choice held up. This is a hypothetical/academic
  project that will never reach that gate — nothing promotes back to `Library/Materials/` from
  this run, and the starter library stays exactly as seeded. Worth naming in the reference itself
  so the next academic-project run doesn't wonder why nothing got promoted.
