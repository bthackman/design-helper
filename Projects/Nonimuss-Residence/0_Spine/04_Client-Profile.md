**Family:** met in their 20s hiking the Rockies; son, 24, away at university; aging parents visit. They value solitude *and* hosting; they cook together; they want bike/walk commutes wherever possible.

## Portrait

**Arnold** is the resident: the house is his workplace, studio, and observatory. He writes in the morning, trains midday, photographs the light in the evening, plays guitar after dinner. His life happens in a 40 m radius, so its quality is everything — the desk's view, the room's acoustics, the path he jogs from the front door.

**Arlene** is the returner: she leaves 18 mornings a month and comes home carrying other people's training loads. Her house works in sequences, not rooms — arrive, shed, swim or sauna or sit in the cold air, then cook with Arnold. Garden and meditation live in the same register: quiet, physical, restorative. The house is her recovery protocol made of wood and water.

**Together** they converge at the kitchen table and on the trail. The house needs exactly one big room — kitchen/dining/living as a single sociable workshop — and it needs to hold a 24-year-old's homecoming and two sets of aging parents without pretending to be bigger than 120 m².

## Contradictions worth designing around
1. **Compact house, expansive life** — sharpened: weights, sauna, cold plunge, bikes, and a pool, at 120 m². Unconditioned satellites do the work; the wellness cluster is the architectural event.
2. **Rural + bikeable** — NEW and site-defining: "rural Canadian site with adjacent water body" *and* bike/walk commutes to NW/SW Calgary. The site search is now a needle-threading exercise (→ OQ-8).
3. **Retreat + gathering** — one room must be both sanctuary for 2 and stage for 8.
4. **Her pool: toward the lake or away?** — carries from v1, enriched: the pool is now part of a swim–sauna–cold sequence, which may answer it (the *sequence* faces the landscape; the lane can be disciplined).
